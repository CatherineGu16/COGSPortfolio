LinkedIn Case Study

A dive into optimizing LinkedIn's job-application design to improve the user experience.

Project type
Class project
Role
Project Manager · UX/UI Designer
Duration
8 weeks
Team
Adam Lamin, Punit Munnangi, Mela Son

Overview

Project Context

LinkedIn is a global leader in job networking and search services, backed by its exceptional reputation; however, we believed we could further enhance LinkedIn's market value and customer satisfaction by improving its job search and application process.

We engaged in two major processes to ensure we created a user-centric design that would truly help LinkedIn succeed — starting with exploratory methods before the redesign, and then various user testing to inform and test the redesign.

Evaluation Methods

Cognitive Walkthrough: what do we think are the issues?

We began our exploration by creating a task: "Find an internship or entry-level job and apply through Easy Apply." We walked through this task as a new user would, and broke it into 12 steps to analyze in detail. At each step we identified potential issues and ranked them by severity to prioritize which to address first.

Competitive Analysis: what do other companies do?

With the issues from the cognitive walkthrough in mind, we turned to LinkedIn's competitors to assess LinkedIn's current effectiveness and propose ways to improve it.

Problem Scope

What problems should we tackle?

From both the Cognitive Walkthrough and the Competitive Analysis, we identified and defined many UI and feature recommendations to tackle in the redesign.

Pre-design User Testing

Card Sort: how should we organize the information architecture?

In our cognitive walkthrough, we identified that features in the current information architecture may be confusing. So we used OptimalWorkshop to conduct a hybrid card sort, letting users move features into the categories they felt were appropriate.

Qualitative Test: are our hypotheses correct?

How did we know the issues we identified were real problems users face? To test our hypothesized issues and stay user-centric, we used UserTesting.com to create think-aloud tasks, then asked a few qualitative questions about the experience.

Post-design User Testing

Quantitative Test: did our changes have the effect we expected?

After building a Figma mockup mimicking the existing LinkedIn flow and another for the new design, we wanted to confirm the redesign was truly more intuitive. We used Maze and Google Forms to run within-subject A/B tests with 29 participants — randomizing which version participants saw first, gathering their opinions on each, and comparing them.

Reflection

What did I learn?

Our team chose the ambitious task of improving such a well-established site, and it was a great experience to work with a mature product rather than one that needed a lot of work. This project let me dive deeper into the user-research side of UX and define our own goals as we moved through the project. I also got to experiment with many research platforms — OptimalWorkshop, UserTesting.com, and Maze. Thank you to my teammates for all your hard work and participation.
